Evidence-Based Fertility Optimization Pillars

1. Mitochondrial & Antioxidant Support

CoQ10, Zinc, Folate, and Omega-3 fatty acids protect both sperm DNA and maturing oocytes from oxidative stress.

2. Scrotal Thermal Regulation

Testicular sperm production requires temperatures 2°C cooler than core body temperature. Frequent breaks prevent heat-induced motility drops.

3. Glycemic & Endocrine Balance

Balanced insulin levels and normalized thyroid/prolactin hormones ensure predictable luteinizing hormone (LH) surges and timely ovulation.

Male Subfertility Factors

Asthenozoospermia: Reduced progressive motility (<30%). Sperm swim too slowly or in aimless directions, making them unable to reach or penetrate the ovum.
Oligozoospermia: Low sperm count (<16 million/mL). Fails to provide the statistical threshold of viable cells required for natural fertilization.
Teratozoospermia: High percentage (>96%) of abnormally shaped sperm (head, midpiece, or tail defects) hindering natural penetration.
Environmental & Thermal Strain: Extended desk sitting (>8 hrs/day) and heat elevate testicular temperatures, impairing motility.

Female Subfertility Factors

Anovulatory PCOS: Imbalanced FSH/LH and high AMH generate multiple underdeveloped follicle clusters that fail to rupture.
Subclinical Thyroid Disruption: TSH levels outside 0.5–2.5 mIU/L alter ovulation timing and prevent optimal LH surges.
Diminished Ovarian Reserve (DOR): Low AMH (<1.0 ng/mL) and minimal Antral Follicle Count indicate declining primordial egg pools.
Tubal & Implantation Factors: Tubal blockages or endometrial linings thinner than 7mm create barriers to natural conception.
